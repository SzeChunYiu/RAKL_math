## Interface 1: finite I does not supply the global critical amplitude

R6 gives a genuine fixed-time local capacity estimate of the form

`m_lambda(R) <= C I M^4 R lambda^-3`

for large observation radius. R7 correctly isolates the monotone-exhaustion obligation: global weak `L^(3/2)` requires radius-uniform distribution control, equivalently a summable annular tail. The new geometric theorem **assumes** that global critical amplitude. Therefore it does not repair the R7 tail gap for generic finite-`I` ancient solutions; it enters downstream of it.

The implication direction must not be reversed. Albritton–Barker note that stronger global critical controls can imply finite `I`; this does not license `finite I => global critical norm`.

## Interface 3: the direction field does not pass through the registered compactness for free

The Albritton–Barker compactness interface gives strong local convergence of velocity in `L^3` (with pressure only weak/localized under the relevant bounds). It does not state strong convergence of `curl u`, nor convergence of

`xi = omega / |omega|`

in weighted BMO.

This is load-bearing. Normalization is singular at `omega=0`; weak derivative convergence can lose direction through oscillation or cancellation. A valid transfer requires a separate lemma specifying at least:

- a convergence mode for vorticity strong enough on the relevant high-vorticity sets;
- control of zeros/defect sets or a quantitative lower bound where direction is consumed;
- stability of the weighted-BMO seminorm under the chosen convergence;
- exact scaling/time uniformity;
- a direction of transfer (original flow -> blow-up limit or limit -> original flow).

No such lemma is present in the registered source package, so the geometric property cannot be glued to the ancient limit by velocity compactness alone.

## Interface 4: original-flow criterion versus ancient-limit rigidity

Grujic's conclusion is a finite-time singularity-evasion criterion for the original solution under the stated profile/amplitude/direction hypotheses. Albritton–Barker's Type-I classification route constructs an ancient limit.

These are different consumers. If the original solution already satisfies Grujic's hypotheses, the geometric theorem applies directly; an ancient bridge is unnecessary. If one tries instead to prove the hypotheses only on the ancient limit, a quantitative back-transfer or Liouville theorem is required. Neither direction is licensed by the current sources.

No backward-uniqueness theorem is invoked here, and none of its hypotheses are silently borrowed.
