## Research method used

The cycle used a source-to-target **critical-element transfer audit** rather than theorem generation. Kenig–Koch and Gallagher–Koch–Planchon were treated as solved source contexts where concentration compactness works in global critical topologies. The target context was the Albritton–Barker finite-`I` Type-I ancient class. `T-XM-ROOT-BRIDGE-STABILITY-AUDIT` forced the missing transfer coordinate to be stated explicitly: does the target scalar control penalize spatial profile splitting strongly enough to support a one-profile critical-element step?

The cheapest hostile world used two fixed smooth compactly supported divergence-free spacetime packets, with pressure set to zero, separated by a distance `L`. This tests only the geometry of the `I` functional.

## What worked

1. **Experience memory changed routing.** `F-NS-B1a1-SCALE-NEUTRAL-LOCAL-ENERGY-LEDGER` and `F-NS-B1a2-KINETIC-ENERGY-NONQUANTIZATION` prevented a third attempt to manufacture an additive scale currency from local energy or global kinetic energy. `F-NS-B1a-C001-PRESSURE-SUMMABILITY` prevented revival of raw far-pressure divergence. `SOURCE-GAP-2606.07875-TERMINAL-SCALE` prevented importing an unverified finite-descent shortcut.
2. **Context-first transfer exposed a hidden assumption.** The source critical-element methods need more than scale invariance; they need usable defect-of-compactness/profile geometry in a global critical setting. The finite-`I` target had not supplied that witness.
3. **Counterexample-first analysis cheaply separated the routes.** A cylinder intersecting both fixed packets must have radius comparable to their separation. Consequently its normalized `A` and `E` terms are `O(L^-1)`, `C` is `O(L^-2)`, and `D=0`. Cylinders seeing only one packet retain a one-packet score, giving asymptotic max-decoupling.
4. **Authority boundaries stayed intact.** The packet is not a Navier–Stokes solution, the result is not a theorem about ancient solutions, and no literature novelty or root progress was claimed.

## What failed

The candidate mechanism

`finite I -> minimize scalar I -> automatic exclusion of spatial dichotomy -> one-profile compact critical element`

fails at the second implication as a consequence of scalar-`I` geometry alone. The scalar value assigns no strict asymptotic extra cost to a second fixed profile translated far away.

This is primarily a **representation / local-to-global / method-transfer failure**, not a local PDE estimate failure. The critical scalar is valid for its source purpose but is insufficient, by itself, as the imported profile-counting currency.

## Local versus global failure

The local cylinder quantities remain meaningful and scale invariant. What fails is their assembly into a global no-dichotomy certificate. This is therefore recorded as a gluing/representation obstruction:

`all local cylinders controlled`

is not the same statement as

`the whole ancient configuration is one tight critical profile modulo symmetries`.

## Concrete framework-improvement hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** method-transfer reviews for compactness/minimal-element arguments should include an explicit `PROFILE_GEOMETRY` coordinate recording whether the target control is additive, orthogonally decoupled, max-like, or backed by a separate no-dichotomy theorem. Scale invariance alone should not satisfy a compactness-transfer precondition.

**Expected benefit:** fewer false transfers of concentration-compactness arguments into local/supremum controls that cannot distinguish one profile from many.

**Possible regression:** an overly rigid additivity requirement could reject valid compactness methods based on non-additive norms or PDE-specific stability.

**Cheapest discriminator:** benchmark the added coordinate on known successful critical-element settings (`Hdot(1/2)`, `L^3`/critical Besov) and hostile local-supremum controls such as the present finite-`I` calibration. Require the check to accept source methods when a valid profile theorem/no-dichotomy certificate is supplied rather than requiring literal additivity.

One Navier–Stokes episode is insufficient to promote this framework change; it remains a proposal to compare with other cross-domain compactness/gluing failures.
