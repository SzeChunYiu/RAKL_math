# NS-B1a2 same-context expert review

**Authority:** same-context role-separated review only. This is not independent peer review and grants no theorem or root authority.

## Shared atom
Test whether finite global kinetic energy can convert Type-I critical `L^3` concentration at `lambda=sqrt(T-t)` into an `O(1)` bound on disjoint critical cores or enough global tail tightness to activate the Albritton–Barker backward-`L^3` Liouville theorem. The hostile audit is intentionally favorable to the route by additionally assuming `||u(t)||_infinity <= M/lambda`.

## Role 1 — PDE / partial-regularity lead
The source-valid bridge remains `finite Type-I control -> Liouville-ready global trace/tail property`; local critical concentration is relevant but is only a lower bound near a putative singularity. Strongest objection: no source theorem converts one local concentration event into global `L^3` compactness. **Vote:** proceed with packing falsifier before any dynamics theorem.

## Role 2 — critical scaling / harmonic-analysis lead
At the `L^3`-critical scale, a velocity blob of radius `lambda` and amplitude `lambda^-1` has `L^3` mass `O(1)` but kinetic energy squared `O(lambda)`. Therefore kinetic energy is not an energy-critical bubble currency. Strongest objection: any finite-energy bubble-count argument must overcome this scaling mismatch. **Vote:** proceed; this is the cheapest discriminator.

## Role 3 — concentration-compactness / profile lead
Kenig–Koch and Gallagher–Koch–Planchon obtain compactness from bounded global critical norms, not from finite `L^2` energy plus local Type-I control. Strongest objection: even a finite number of cores at each scale would not by itself prove orbit precompactness, and an `O(lambda^-1)` bound is far weaker. **Vote:** proceed and keep critical-element minimality as a distinct future route.

## Role 4 — vorticity / geometry lead
Finite energy contains no orientation/coherence information. A negative packing result must not be interpreted as evidence against vorticity-alignment or depletion mechanisms. **Vote:** proceed, with geometry preserved as a live alternative.

## Role 5 — adversarial falsification lead
Use the strongest convenient assumptions: finite energy, pointwise Type-I amplitude, and pairwise disjoint critical balls. If the resulting count still diverges, the instantaneous energy-quantization route is structurally insufficient. Then construct disjoint divergence-free scaled bumps to show the exponents can be saturated without claiming a Navier–Stokes solution. **Vote:** proceed.

## Role 6 — formal-methods / authority lead
The prospective result can only be a route-pruning analytic lemma about consequences of explicitly stated inequalities. The divergence-free packed snapshot is a functional calibration, not a PDE counterexample. Keep the favorable pointwise Type-I amplitude assumption explicit because it is stronger than finite Albritton–Barker `I`. **Vote:** proceed with candidate generation after the pre-candidate trace freezes.

## Role 7 — novelty / research-value lead
The potentially useful content is the explicit scale mismatch: unlike energy-critical bubbling, every `L^3`-critical Navier–Stokes velocity core costs only `O(lambda)` kinetic energy, so finite energy cannot quantize critical profile count. Treat this as route normalization unless a novelty search later shows otherwise. **Vote:** proceed, no novelty claim.

No role argues that the packing calculation decides the Type-I problem. The only disagreement is strategic: the profile lead ranks minimal-critical-element compactness as the likely next route, while the geometry lead prefers coherence/depletion. Both agree that finite kinetic energy should first be tested and, if pruned, not recycled under a different name.
