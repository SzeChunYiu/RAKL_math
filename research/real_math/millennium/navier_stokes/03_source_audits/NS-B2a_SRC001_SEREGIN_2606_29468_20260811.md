## Exact scaling and PDE-class change

In the registered Type-II branch, Seregin uses a scaling of the form

- `x = λ y`,
- `t = λ^2 f(λ) τ`,
- `v^λ(y,τ) = λ f(λ) v(x,t)`,
- `q^λ(y,τ) = λ^2 f(λ)^2 q(x,t)`.

Substitution into Navier–Stokes gives a rescaled viscous term with coefficient `f(λ)`. In the branch where `f(λ) -> 0`, the viscosity disappears in the blow-up limit. The limiting profile governed by Theorem 3.1 is therefore an ancient incompressible **Euler** object, not an ancient Navier–Stokes object.

This is load-bearing: any rigidity closure that requires a uniformly parabolic limiting equation is disallowed unless a separate theorem restores those hypotheses.

## Source-bound extraction interface

For the F=1 branch used here, the theorem/proof provides the following interface, and no stronger one is assumed:

1. a rescaled sequence of suitable weak Navier–Stokes solutions;
2. local compactness sufficient to extract a velocity limit on bounded space-time cylinders;
3. locally normalized/controlled pressure with weak local convergence at the natural pressure exponent;
4. passage to an ancient Euler system as the rescaled viscosity tends to zero;
5. inheritance of the source scale-invariant local estimate labelled `(3.5)`;
6. a local-energy inequality in the limiting class;
7. a source nontriviality lower bound labelled `(3.8)`.

The audit deliberately distinguishes local strong velocity convergence from global spatial tightness. Local compactness cannot prevent translated/dilated mass or energy from escaping every fixed ball.

## Retrospective calibration consequence of (3.5)

**Chronology:** this calculation was performed after reading the source but before freezing `NS-B2a`. It is therefore a retrospective diagnostic/source consequence with **zero preregistration or candidate-promotion credit**.

In the F=1 branch, the gradient part of `(3.5)` has the schematic scale-invariant bound

`(1/a) ∫_{-a^2}^0 ∫_{B(a)} |∇u|^2 <= C`.

Hence

`∫_{-a^2}^0 ∫_{B(a)} |∇u|^2 <= C a`.

Averaging over the early half-window `(-a^2,-a^2/2)` gives a time `τ_a` in that interval with

`∫_{B(a)} |∇u(x,τ_a)|^2 dx <= 2C/a`.

The A-type term in the same source bound gives at that time

`∫_{B(a)} |u(x,τ_a)|^2 dx <= C a`.

Thus the spatial mean over `B(a)` satisfies `|ū_{B(a)}(τ_a)| <= C/a`. Scale-invariant Sobolev–Poincaré then yields

`||u(τ_a)-ū_{B(a)}(τ_a)||_{L^6(B(a))} <= C ||∇u(τ_a)||_{L^2(B(a))} <= C a^(-1/2)`.

For every fixed `R`, once `a>R`,

`||u(τ_a)||_{L^6(B(R))} -> 0` as `a -> infinity`, while `τ_a -> -infinity`.

Therefore local `L^3(B(R))` also tends to zero along that backward sequence.

### What the calibration does not prove

It does **not** imply `u≡0`. The source class does not provide the missing bridge from local backward smallness to global rigidity:

- global `L^2` can be infinite; the source A-bound permits energy growing with radius;
- energy or critical mass may sit outside `B(a)` and later enter;
- Euler pressure is nonlocal;
- local convergence does not control profile leakage;
- standard Navier–Stokes backward uniqueness is unavailable.

## Adversarial construction audit: compactly supported steady Euler

A nonzero compactly supported steady Euler field is a natural adversarial test because it is ancient, smooth, and spatially localized. It is **not** a counterexample to the F=1 Seregin class. For a nonzero steady field,

`∫_{-a^2}^0 ∫_{B(a)} |∇u|^2 ~ a^2`

for all sufficiently large `a` once `B(a)` contains the support. The `(1/a)` prefactor in the F=1 gradient term then produces growth of order `a`, violating a uniform `(3.5)` bound. This falsifier is rejected for a precise target-specific reason rather than by appeal to general Euler rigidity.

## Exact theorem still needed

A closure theorem must have hypotheses no stronger than properties actually inherited from the Navier–Stokes blow-up sequence. The desired interface is:

> Let `(u,p)` be an ancient Euler solution belonging to the exact Seregin Theorem 3.1 F=1 limit class, satisfying `(3.5)`, the inherited local-energy inequality, source nontriviality bookkeeping, and the backward expanding-ball smallness consequence above. Suppose in addition that a **target-specific no-incoming-energy/far-field-tightness condition**, proved directly from the original suitable weak Navier–Stokes blow-up sequence, passes to this limit. Then `u≡0`.

The two open load-bearing atoms are therefore:

1. prove the no-incoming-energy/far-field condition from the original Type-II Navier–Stokes sequence and pass it through the singular zero-viscosity scaling; and
2. prove Euler rigidity in exactly that inherited class.
