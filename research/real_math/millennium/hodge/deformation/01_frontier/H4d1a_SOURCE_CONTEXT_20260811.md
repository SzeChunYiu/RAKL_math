## Exact scope

The root target remains the rational Hodge conjecture for smooth projective complex algebraic varieties. H4d1a is much narrower: it starts with a chosen algebraic witness on one fiber and asks only about propagating that witness along a branch of the Hodge locus. It cannot address initial algebraicity of an arbitrary rational Hodge class.

## Parent result and child atom

The H4d1 witness-projection calibration separated four non-interchangeable arrows:

`Hodge-locus compatibility -> witness lifting -> algebraization -> global continuation`.

Its hostile control exposed a precise gap. If a semiregularity/Hodge detector is not faithful on the geometric obstruction directions generated by the chosen branch, Hodge persistence can at most place an obstruction in the detector kernel; it does not force the geometric obstruction itself to vanish.

H4d1a asks whether full semiregularity can be replaced by a genuinely weaker, independently checkable condition. For each relevant Artin order `m`, seek a geometrically defined envelope

`B_{T,m} subset Ob_m`

such that all obstructions generated by lifts along the fixed Hodge-locus branch `T` lie in `B_{T,m}`, while the detector is injective on `B_{T,m}`. The phrase “relevant obstruction” is forbidden unless the relevant subobject is defined independently of the desired vanishing.

## Current primary-source map

### Buchweitz–Flenner — semiregularity as an obstruction-theoretic map

Buchweitz and Flenner construct a general semiregularity map for algebraic cycles/perfect complexes and relate it to Hilbert-scheme deformation questions and the variational Hodge conjecture. The construction supplies a legitimate source mechanism for mapping geometric obstruction theory into Hodge/cohomological data, but it does not say that an arbitrary branch-restricted subspace is automatically faithful.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9912245

### Pridham — derived reduced obstruction theory

Pridham realizes the Buchweitz–Flenner semiregularity map as the tangent of a morphism of derived moduli functors and derives a global reduced obstruction theory. This is the strongest source analogue for replacing a full obstruction theory by a smaller one. The transfer obligation for H4d1a is stricter: the reduced object must be tied to the chosen Hodge branch and its faithfulness must be proved on the branch-generated directions, not inferred from terminology.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.6001

### Nishinou — branch-relative obstruction from ambient deformation

Nishinou studies relative deformations of semiregular maps whose images are divisors and, under the source hypotheses, relates relative deformability to persistence of the cycle class as Hodge. The useful structural coordinate is that the ambient Kodaira–Spencer direction produces a concrete relative geometric obstruction before semiregularity is used to detect it. H4d1a asks whether the image of the allowed branch directions can be characterized before imposing full semiregularity.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01651

### Kloosterman — a special family where geometric and Hodge loci can be compared

Kloosterman proves the variational Hodge conjecture for complete-intersection cycles on hypersurfaces in projective space under the stated degree conditions. His proof compares a geometric flag-Hilbert locus with the Hodge locus through explicit tangent-space and dimension/smoothness calculations. This is a positive source control for independently computing allowed geometric directions in a special family; it is not an all-order branch-envelope theorem for arbitrary cycles.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14845

### Liu–Shen — explicit local equations for Hodge-preserving directions

Liu and Shen give an analytic description of Hodge loci in Kuranishi families and a Beltrami-differential criterion for variational-Hodge questions. The useful transfer is only on the base side: explicit equations can help specify the allowed directions of `T`. They do not themselves construct an algebraic-cycle witness or prove that a witness obstruction vanishes.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13951

## Perspective scan

The active obstruction was viewed through six deliberately different lenses.

1. **VHS/base geometry:** compute the tangent/Artin directions allowed by the Hodge condition.
2. **cycle-moduli geometry:** determine the witness deformation functor and its actual obstruction object.
3. **derived obstruction theory:** seek a reduced obstruction object that is defined functorially before success is known.
4. **reachability/control abstraction:** treat branch directions as restricted inputs and geometric obstructions as reachable error states.
5. **adversarial boundary geometry:** search for non-semiregular examples where branch restriction is visibly smaller than the full obstruction space.
6. **coefficient/globalization audit:** keep rational signed cycles, algebraization, monodromy, and global continuation outside the local detector claim.

The anti-collapse result is that “injective on relevant obstructions” is not a candidate theorem. It is only a template. H4d1a needs an independently constructed `B_{T,m}` and an old-failure regression against the detector kernel.

## Cheapest discriminator selected before candidate generation

After the strict packet passes, the first bounded action is a **strict-weaker non-semiregular control**. It must exhibit a smooth-projective family and chosen witness for which:

- the full obstruction space is nonzero;
- the full semiregularity detector is not injective;
- the branch-generated envelope is independently forced to be strictly smaller;
- witness lifting along the branch is checked directly;
- the result is labelled a boundary calibration if the envelope is zero.

A zero-envelope control can establish logical separation from full semiregularity, but it does not yet demonstrate the coupled nonzero mechanism needed for meaningful progress. Success therefore opens, rather than closes, the next residual: find `0 < B_{T,m} < Ob_m` in a coupled family and test stability at the next Artin order.
