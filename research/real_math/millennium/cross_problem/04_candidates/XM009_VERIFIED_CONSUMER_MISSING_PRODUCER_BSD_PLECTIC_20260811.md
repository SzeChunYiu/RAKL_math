## Transfer contract

### Common abstraction

Let `P` be a root-facing producer condition, `W` an intermediate witness, and `C` a verified consumer:

```text
P(x)  ?->  W
W      ->  C(W)
```

Verifying that `C` is stable, well-typed, or powerful **does not reverse the first arrow**.

### Enabling assumptions

1. The source lesson is used only as search control, never as BSD evidence.
2. The BSD producer must start from the exact complex analytic-rank-two datum and preserve the target elliptic-curve/field/hypothesis scope.
3. The produced plectic object must be the same object, or be linked by a proved comparison, to the object consumed by the downstream rank theorem.
4. The theorem direction must be producer-to-witness; a converse implication is not accepted.
5. Any auxiliary `p`, quadratic field, reduction, sign, or local-condition hypotheses remain explicit and cannot be erased.

### Disanalogies

- Navier–Stokes uses geometric scaling and blow-up limits; BSD uses complex and p-adic L-functions, Selmer groups, and auxiliary arithmetic choices.
- The NS consumer stability is an elementary scaling identity; the BSD producer is a deep arithmetic nonvanishing problem.
- A PDE global/far-field gluing failure has no direct arithmetic content.
- Rank-one classical Heegner-point theorems need not extrapolate to rank-two plectic objects.

### DifferenceWitness

The cheapest internal positive/negative control is the **rank-one versus higher-rank plectic boundary**.

Fornea–Gehrmann's polyquadratic plectic paper records that in the one-prime case the plectic point is a classical Heegner point, and Proposition 3.1 gives an analytic-rank-one/nonzero-point equivalence under its stated character setting. In contrast, the higher-rank plectic significance is presented through conjectures and indirect evidence. Thus the rank-one producer theorem is a valid positive control but is not a rank-two producer theorem.

This witness blocks the invalid analogy:

```text

therefore
rank 2 plectic object has producer theorem
```
