## C025-L1 — joint-signature calibration

Let

\[
\Lambda=\{(E_1,U\setminus E_1),\ldots,(E_k,U\setminus E_k)\}
\]

be normalized partition pairs. Define the joint signature of diagonal witness `d_u` by

\[
\sigma_\Lambda(u)_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&d_u\in E_i,\\
0,&d_u\notin E_i.
\end{cases}
\]

If `Lambda` covers every canonical semi-filter `F_(u,v)` with `u != v`, then `sigma_Lambda` is injective.

### Proof

Fix `u != v`. By Claim 41, for a normalized partition pair to cover `F_(u,v)`, the singleton witnesses `d_u` and `d_v` must lie on opposite sides of that partition. Since the full family covers `F_(u,v)`, some coordinate `i` separates them, so

\[
\sigma_\Lambda(u)_i\ne \sigma_\Lambda(v)_i.
\]

Thus every two distinct vertices have distinct joint signatures. Hence

\[
N\le 2^k,\qquad k\ge \lceil\log_2 N\rceil=n.
\]

Conversely, label `[N]` by distinct `n`-bit strings and let `E_i` contain precisely the diagonal witnesses whose `i`th label bit is `1`. Every `u != v` differs in some coordinate, so Claim 41 shows that the corresponding partition pair covers `F_(u,v)`. Therefore the canonical joint-signature calibration is exactly

\[
k_{\rm sig}(G_{\rm NEQ})=n=\log_2 N.
\]

This is an alternative encoding of the canonical part of Proposition 40, not a new lower-bound theorem.
