## 2. Source-bound comparison

### Main text: variable lattice time

Theorem 5.4 defines `Delta_k = -(1/a_k) log ||T_k|Q_k||` for a one-step transfer `T_k`, with `a_(k+1)=b a_k`, and derives `||T_(k+1)|Q_(k+1)|| <= exp(-b a_k Delta_k) + ||E_k||`. Its exact logarithmic consequence is

`Delta_(k+1) >= Delta_k - exp(a_(k+1) Delta_k) ||E_k|| / a_(k+1)`.

That exact formula is the right starting point. The following simplification in the source, from `Delta_k >= 0` to `exp(a_(k+1)Delta_k) <= exp(a_(k+1))`, requires an additional upper bound such as `Delta_k <= 1`; nonnegativity alone does not imply it. Likewise a geometric sequence `a_k=b^k a_0` is not uniformly comparable to one for unbounded `k` without an explicitly restricted scale range or changed normalization.

### Appendix D: fixed physical time

Appendix D instead begins with fixed `tau>0` and defines `T_(k,L) = exp(-tau H_(k,L))` on a finite-volume gauge-invariant OS Hilbert space. It defines `delta_(k,L) = 1 - ||T_(k,L)|Q_(k,L)||` and `m_(k,L) = -tau^(-1) log(1-delta_(k,L))`. It then introduces a vacuum-preserving scale isometry `Pi_(k,L)` and a same-`tau` defect `E_(k,L) = T_(k+1,L) - Pi_(k,L) T_(k,L) Pi_(k,L)^*` with a summable volume-uniform norm hypothesis/bound.

This fixed-`tau` normalization avoids the explicit `exp(a Delta)/a` conversion loss **inside that local setup**. It does not by itself prove that the Appendix-D `T_(k,L)` is the same semigroup element as the main-text one-step transfer, or that its scale embedding is the same RG map after changing time normalization.

## 3. Exact local spectral lemma that is safe

Let `H>=0` be self-adjoint, let the vacuum be the zero-energy eigenspace generated by `Omega`, and let `Q` be its orthogonal complement. If `S(tau)=exp(-tau H)` on the same Hilbert space, spectral calculus gives `||S(tau)|Q|| = exp(-tau m)` whenever `m = inf sigma(H|Q)`. Hence `m = -tau^(-1) log ||S(tau)|Q|| = -tau^(-1)log(1-delta_tau)`.

This is an exact normalization identity. It is **conditional on a common Hamiltonian and common excited-space projection**; it cannot manufacture that common generator from two separately defined transfer families.

## 4. The missing same-theory interface

A fixed-`tau` replacement is licensed only after proving all of the following.

1. **Hilbert-space identity.** The main-text `H_k` and Appendix-D `H_(k,L)` are either literally the same finite-volume OS construction at matching parameters or are related by exact, source-bound unitaries/isometries that preserve vacuum and physical gauge-invariant sectors.
2. **Generator identity.** There is one self-adjoint `H_(k,L)` such that `T^(a)_(k,L)=exp(-a_k H_(k,L))` and `S^(tau)_(k,L)=exp(-tau H_(k,L))`.
3. **Semigroup/time conversion.** If `tau=n_k a_k` with integer `n_k`, then `S^(tau)=(T^(a))^n_k`. If `tau/a_k` is not integral, an already-constructed strongly continuous semigroup or an exact interpolation theorem is required. A discrete transfer operator alone does not license arbitrary real powers as a physical time translation without generator control.
4. **RG intertwining at common physical time.** The scale map must compare `S^(tau)_(k+1,L)` with the embedded `S^(tau)_(k,L)`, not silently compare operators corresponding to different physical times. The embedding must preserve the vacuum and relevant excited space and be compatible with the generator/semigroup identification.
5. **Defect conversion.** For contractions `||A^n-B^n|| <= n ||A-B||`. Thus converting a one-step error to fixed `tau` with `n≈tau/a_k` can amplify it by `1/a_k`. A claim of `a`-uniform fixed-time control needs source-valid compensation: a sufficiently small one-step defect in `a`, a Duhamel/generator estimate, or a directly proved fixed-time kernel bound.
6. **Relative gap budget.** Even a summable defect sequence must satisfy the quantitative margin required by issue `#73`; summability alone is not a positive residual certificate.
7. **Thermodynamic and continuum compatibility.** Bounds uniform in spatial volume `L` must also be uniform on the continuum/RG path. The limiting OS state, Hilbert space, and Hamiltonian must be identified strongly enough that the lower spectral bound survives.
8. **Physical source completeness.** If the final gap is inferred from correlation decay rather than direct spectral convergence, the same-theory source family must satisfy the density/cyclicity/common-rate conditions isolated in `EP-YM-S1A1-DENSE-SOURCE-GAP-20260811`.

The bounded source locations inspected establish pieces of this list, but not the whole interface as a single bound chain. The cycle therefore fails closed at gluing.

## 5. Counterexample-first hostile controls

**H1 — normalization mismatch.** Two positive contractions can have the same transfer-gap number while representing `exp(-tau H)` for different `tau` or different `H`. The number alone does not identify a physical mass.

**H2 — power amplification.** For contractions, `A^n-B^n = sum_(j=0)^(n-1) A^j(A-B)B^(n-1-j)`, so `||A^n-B^n|| <= n||A-B||`. If `n=tau/a`, a fixed one-step error scale can become nonuniform as `a->0`.

**H3 — unrelated-Hilbert-space interlacing.** Appendix D's abstract interlacing lemma remains true for many positive contractions and vacuum-preserving isometries that are not a Yang–Mills RG trajectory. Validity of the local lemma does not prove same-theory gluing.

**H4 — hidden spectral sector.** Restricted-source clustering can coexist with a lower hidden excitation. The earlier YM spectral-visibility episode is retained as a boundary condition on any correlation-decay route.

**H5 — defect budget.** `delta_0=1`, `epsilon_0=0.6`, `epsilon_1=0.6` and later zero defects are summable but do not leave a positive telescoping margin. This is the sibling obstruction `#73`.

All five controls survive; none falsifies the local spectral calculus identity. They falsify only unlicensed gluing shortcuts.
