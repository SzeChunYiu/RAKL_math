## Primary-source scope audit

### 1. Background-field/variational layer

T. Bałaban, *The variational problem and background fields in renormalization group method for lattice gauge theories*, Commun. Math. Phys. **102** (1985) 277–309, DOI `10.1007/BF01229381`.

Source scope retained: for regular lattice gauge fields with fixed averages, the action minimization problem has a minimum unique up to gauge transformations, and the resulting background field is the basis for the expansion/perturbative RG analysis.

**What transfers:** an actual weak-coupling marked block should be formulated around the same coarse-variable/background-field geometry as the unmarked step.

**What does not transfer automatically:** uniqueness/control of the unmarked background field gives no closure theorem for differentiated source insertions.

### 2. Four-dimensional small-field one-step RG

T. Bałaban, *Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories. I. Generation of effective actions in a small field approximation and a coupling constant renormalization in four dimensions*, Commun. Math. Phys. **109** (1987) 249–301, DOI `10.1007/BF01215223`.

Source scope retained: four-dimensional pure gauge theory in the small-field approximation; localized effective actions are generated by cluster expansions in one-step renormalization transformations, with beta functions/coupling renormalization defined recursively.

**What transfers:** the child marked space should be tested against the same one-step localization/blocking structure and the running weak coupling, not against an unrelated exact Haar elimination.

**What does not transfer automatically:** preservation/control of the unmarked effective-action class does not imply preservation/control of source tangents or composite/loop insertions.

### 3. Cluster-expansion closure of the unmarked step

T. Bałaban, *Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories. II. Cluster expansions*, Commun. Math. Phys. **116** (1988) 1–22, DOI `10.1007/BF01239022`.

Source scope retained: the fluctuation-field integral introduced in Part I is represented by an exponentiated cluster expansion and its terms satisfy the inductive assumptions, completing the small-field effective-action sequence.

**What transfers:** marked observables should be organized at the same localization/polymer granularity if one wants estimates compatible with the base RG induction.

**What does not transfer automatically:** a cluster expansion for the density does not prove that a marked derivative stays in a finite observable vocabulary; the mark may proliferate across polymer geometry, representations, and connected components.

### 4. Large-field operation and four-dimensional ultraviolet stability

T. Bałaban, *Large field renormalization. I. The basic step of the R operation*, Commun. Math. Phys. **122** (1989) 175–202, DOI `10.1007/BF01257412`.

T. Bałaban, *Large field renormalization. II. Localization, exponentiation, and bounds for the R operation*, Commun. Math. Phys. **122** (1989) 355–392, DOI `10.1007/BF01238433`.

Source scope retained: the `R` operation treats large-field regions; the second paper states that the basic properties/bounds complete the proof of ultraviolet stability of four-dimensional pure gauge field theories at the level formulated there.

**What transfers:** a credible marked space must eventually coexist with the small/large-field split rather than live only on a globally small-field event.

**What does not transfer automatically:** ultraviolet stability/normalization control of the partition-function/effective-density sector is not a construction of the continuum observable algebra, OS Hilbert space, or mass gap.
